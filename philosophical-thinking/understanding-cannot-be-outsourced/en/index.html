AI Cannot Deliver Understanding Directly

August 31, 2026

As I use large language models more and more, a line I once heard—though I no longer remember where—has grown increasingly clear in my mind:

“You can outsource thinking, but you cannot outsource understanding.”

Only later, when I looked it up, did I discover that Andrej Karpathy had also emphasized this idea in an interview this April.

Indeed, an LLM can carry out part of the thinking process for us, but it cannot simply hand us understanding. Thinking and understanding are inseparable: when we use AI to take shortcuts in thinking, our understanding of what AI has organized for us often becomes shallower as a result.

AI has dramatically expanded the bandwidth of thought, but human bandwidth for understanding has not increased accordingly. Perhaps this explains why many people feel that AI has not really made work easier: tasks move faster, but the cognitive burden of absorbing, judging, and taking responsibility has not diminished.

In the early stage of Agent Harnesses becoming widespread, AI can easily create the illusion that our capabilities have been multiplied N-fold. We may suddenly seem capable of anything, but without running into walls ourselves, experiencing friction, and thinking things through repeatedly, our understanding may remain hollow. Once outside AI’s context window, our actual capabilities are laid bare.

Therefore, as a non-technical person, Agentic AI currently serves me primarily as a tool for accelerating research and turning ideas into reality. It can help me complete tasks faster, but none of the thinking, understanding, or judgment required along the way can be skipped.
